# Austral Beacon AI Lab — Brand Guidelines

This document defines the editorial, visual and strategic identity rules for Austral Beacon AI Lab and the connected media ecosystem.

The goal is to keep all AI-generated outputs aligned with a documentary, cartographic, geographic and evidence-based identity.

---

## Brand Ecosystem

### Parent / Infrastructure Brand

## Austral Beacon

Austral Beacon is the long-term parent brand and technical/media infrastructure layer.

It should represent:

- southern intelligence
- editorial infrastructure
- AI-assisted research
- geographic documentation
- Antarctic and Patagonian knowledge systems
- media technology
- cartographic storytelling

Austral Beacon should feel serious, technical, sober and trustworthy.

---

### Core Documentary / Cartographic Product

## End of the World Atlas

End of the World Atlas is the main documentary and cartographic product.

It should focus on:

- maps
- geography
- routes
- places
- lighthouses
- maritime corridors
- southern infrastructure
- Puerto Williams
- Cape Horn
- Tierra del Fuego
- Patagonia
- Antarctic gateway routes

Tone: documentary, geographic, educational, cinematic, precise.

Avoid: generic travel-blog tone, influencer aesthetics, exaggerated adventure clichés.

---

### Travel-Facing Brand

## End of the World Travel

End of the World Travel is the more public-facing travel and social layer.

It should focus on:

- visual discovery
- routes
- practical travel context
- southern landscapes
- gateway narratives
- Patagonia and Antarctic access
- bilingual content potential

Avoid: empty tourism slogans, generic influencer-style captions, overpromising experiences.

- cartographic
- evidence-based
- geographic

- sober
- precise

- sensationalist media

## Visual Direction

Preferred visual language:

- cold colors
- oceanic blues
- ice tones
- deep green
- muted orange accents
- map lines
- coordinates
- maritime charts
- topographic textures
- satellite-inspired layouts
- lighthouses
- routes
- radar / pulse motifs
- documentary photography

Avoid:

- overly tropical travel colors
- generic beach tourism visuals
- cartoonish Antarctica clichés
- excessive flags
- aggressive national symbols
- low-quality AI imagery
- fake-looking expedition visuals

---

## Typography Direction

Preferred typography:

- clean sans-serif
- editorial serif for longform sections if needed
- technical labels
- map-style metadata
- coordinates and small uppercase labels

The typography should feel:

- modern
- serious
- readable
- geographic
- technical but not coldly corporate

---

## Writing Style

Preferred writing style:

- clear
- precise
- contextual
- explanatory
- careful with claims
- source-aware
- documentary
- accessible but not simplistic

Avoid:

- hype
- clickbait
- exaggerated mystery
- empty slogans
- nationalist overclaiming
- unverified statements
- excessive emojis in serious contexts

---

## Language Strategy

Primary languages:

- Spanish for regional reach
- English for global SEO and international positioning

Recommended workflow:

1. Draft in the language best suited to the audience.
2. Preserve source meaning.
3. Translate carefully, not literally.
4. Adapt SEO terms by language.
5. Keep geographic names consistent.
6. Use bilingual outputs when strategically useful.

---

## AI Usage Rules

AI may be used for:

- summaries
- classification
- outlines
- metadata
- SEO drafts
- translation drafts
- social post drafts
- RAG source preparation
- internal research notes
- workflow documentation

AI should not be used to:

- invent facts
- invent sources
- fabricate quotes
- fabricate locations
- publish without review
- generate misleading images
- replace human verification
- make legal, scientific or historical claims without sources

---

## Source Discipline

Every serious output should preserve:

- source URL
- source name
- publication date if available
- author if available
- type of source
- verification status
- notes about uncertainty

Source quality hierarchy:

1. Official institutional sources
2. Scientific / academic sources
3. Primary documents
4. Reputable media
5. Specialist blogs or local sources
6. Tourism/commercial sources
7. User notes
8. Unverified sources

## Brand Risk Checklist

Before publishing or generating public content, check:

- Is the claim sourced?
- Is the tone too sensational?
- Is the Chilean perspective presented with evidence rather than propaganda?
- Is this too generic?
- Does it fit the correct brand?
- Is it clear whether the content is draft, research or publication?
- Are geographic names accurate?
- Are sources preserved?
- Is AI being used responsibly?
