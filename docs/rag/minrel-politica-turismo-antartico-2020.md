## Six policy principles

1. Environmentally responsible, non-invasive Antarctic tourism subject to environmental-impact evaluation.
2. Permanent operational and environmental safety framework.
3. Tourism as a vehicle for education about Antarctic natural heritage, Chile's historical/geographical links and the Antarctic Treaty System.
4. Strengthen Magallanes and Chilean Antarctica as a platform for Antarctic tourism through services and operational/logistics infrastructure.
5. Promote and require participation of national Antarctic tourism operators in IAATO.
6. Ensure effective coordination between public and private actors.

## High-value findings for Austral Beacon

### Punta Arenas and Puerto Williams as gateways

The policy explicitly highlights **Punta Arenas and Puerto Williams as gateways and bridges to Antarctica**. This is directly relevant to Antarctica Begins and the Austral Connectivity Observatory.

The policy also argues that geographic proximity alone is insufficient: gateway positioning requires competitive services, Antarctic identity/culture, scientific and historical narratives, infrastructure and logistics.

### Magallanes as tourism platform

The policy treats the Región de Magallanes y de la Antártica Chilena as a platform for Antarctic tourism and calls for development of related services and operational/logistics infrastructure.

This provides a direct policy predecessor to Objective 5 of the Plan Estratégico Antártico 2026–2030, which later frames Magallanes as a scientific, technological, logistical, cultural and tourism development hub.

### Aero-cruise model

The policy specifically recognizes the growth of the **aero-cruise** modality, where part of the Antarctic journey occurs by air and continues by sea. This is strategically important for documenting Chilean alternatives to conventional sea-only departures.

The claim should be used as historical/policy context. Current products, schedules and operators must be verified with contemporary first-party sources such as DAP and Antarctica21.

### Aeródromo Teniente Marsh

The document identifies year-round air connectivity through Aeródromo Teniente Marsh as a strength for Antarctic tourism through Chile.

This creates a clear continuity with the 2026–2030 Plan, which includes runway conservation and passenger-infrastructure actions for the Marsh airfield.

### Search and Rescue

The policy states that Chile has Search and Rescue (SAR) responsibility in an assigned Antarctic area and connects this responsibility with the need to develop infrastructure, services, coordination and contingency capabilities.

This should be connected in the Knowledge Layer to aviation, maritime logistics, emergency response and operator requirements.

### Environmental control

Tourism is framed as an activity that must be controlled and sustainable. The policy emphasizes:

- environmental-impact evaluation;
- contingency planning and mitigation;
- protected areas and visitor-site guidance;
- precaution where impacts are uncertain;
- visitor capacity considerations;
- national and Antarctic Treaty System rules.

### IAATO

The policy promotes and requires participation of national Antarctic tourism operators in IAATO and argues that companies operating from Chile should participate in the association to align with international standards and information practices.

## Important temporal rule
