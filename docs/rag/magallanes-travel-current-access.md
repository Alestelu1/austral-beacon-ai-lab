# Magallanes Travel — current access verification

Checked: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

Separate stable territorial knowledge from current transport, road-construction and visitor-service status for the End of the World Travel Assistant and related Austral Beacon products.

## Yendegaia

Current CONAF information states that Parque Nacional Yendegaia has no visitor attention and no public-use infrastructure. CONAF nevertheless describes two territorial approaches: maritime access from Puerto Williams/Puerto Navarino toward Bahía Yendegaia and a terrestrial approach through Ruta Y-85 to Lago Fagnano and the Vicuña–Yendegaia penetration road.

This distinction is mandatory for retrieval: an access description does not imply a staffed entrance, visitor facilities, scheduled transport or safe/general public availability.

## Vicuña–Yendegaia / Ruta Y-85

Current 2026 evidence from Ejército de Chile / Cuerpo Militar del Trabajo shows active Etapa XI works, including construction activity in the Cordillera Darwin front and installation of a modular bridge in July 2026. MOP project data also retains future execution allocations.

Safe interpretation: the corridor remains under construction. Do not tell travelers that a continuous road from the Chilean mainland to Puerto Williams is complete.

## Punta Arenas–Puerto Williams maritime service

TABSA currently publishes Punta Arenas–Puerto Williams as an active maritime route and describes the crossing as approximately 32 hours. This replaces the 31-hour travel time printed in the older Ruta de los Parques road map for current-answer purposes.

Dates, fares, vessel assignment and availability are live/booking-sensitive and must be checked at answer time.

TABSA's current Puerto Williams operations listing includes Punta Arenas, Puerto Williams, Puerto Toro and Caleta 2 de Mayo in its service scope. This does not by itself establish a fixed-frequency sailing to Caleta 2 de Mayo on any specific date.

## Retrieval policy

Use the following priority order for travel answers:

1. Current institutional access/closure status (CONAF, MOP, CMT where applicable).
2. Current first-party transport operator data (TABSA and other operators).
3. Stable canonical geography/entities.
4. Historical/specialist maps and brochures for context only.

Any conflict between an old map and a current first-party/operator source should resolve in favor of the newer authoritative evidence, while preserving the historical source in provenance.
